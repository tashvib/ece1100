Biography

A little about me and my background.

I'm Tashvi Bansal, a first-year Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech who loves working where AI meets hardware. I've co-authored research on making large language models reason more faithfully, designed digital logic in SystemVerilog, and shipped an iOS app, and I'm looking for a Summer 2027 internship in AI/ML hardware acceleration, digital design, or explainable AI.

My path into engineering started with building things: an adventure game coded from scratch, and GreenOvation, an award-winning iOS app that turns waste sorting and sustainability education into a game. Those projects taught me how much I enjoy taking an idea all the way from a sketch to something people can actually use.

From there I moved into research. I co-authored Follow My Lead, a paper on neurosymbolic methods that improved LLM logical reasoning by 20.7% without expensive retraining, and presented it at Advances in Cognitive Systems at Georgia Tech. Earlier projects had me building NLP pipelines to study media narratives and transformer models to predict community homelessness, and presenting that work to rooms of 150+ researchers.

I also care about making technology accessible. I helped build Mitra, a low-cost Raspberry Pi tutoring device for students without reliable internet, led summer coding camps as a Code Coach, and have tutored learners across five countries.

At Georgia Tech, I want to go deeper into the hardware side of AI: how chips and digital systems can make intelligent software faster, cheaper, and more trustworthy.
