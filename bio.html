Biography

I'm Tashvi Bansal, a Computer Engineering student at the Georgia Institute of Technology with experience in digital hardware design, computer architecture, and artificial intelligence research. My work includes RTL design and verification, processor implementation, formal reasoning, and neurosymbolic AI.

I have conducted research at the Artificial Intelligence Explainability and Accountability (AIEA) Lab under Professor Leilani Gilpin, where I co-authored two papers examining the reliability and reasoning capabilities of large language models. The research I co-authored on LLM-based legal reasoning was accepted to the AI4Law and AI for Math workshops at ICML 2026 and identified limitations in formal reasoning across five language models. In separate research on neurosymbolic AI, I aided in developing a Prolog-based approach that improved logical fallacy classification accuracy by 20.7% without additional model training. I presented this work at the 2025 Advances in Cognitive Systems Conference and Bay Area Machine Learning Symposium.

My hardware experience includes designing and implementing a RISC-V processor in SystemVerilog, with work spanning RTL development, functional verification, synthesis, physical design, and timing analysis using Cadence and Synopsys tools for the Silicon Jackets club onboarding process at Georgia Tech.

Beyond research and hardware development, I developed GreenOvation, an iOS application using computer vision for waste classification and environmental impact analysis. The application reached over 100 users, won “Most Creative Hack” at PoweringSTEMHacks2, and placed 3rd in the Congressional App Challenge for district OR-06.
